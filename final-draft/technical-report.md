## Introduction
As trends and weird events happen on the internet, it can be difficult to keep up with the rapid pace of culture. Fortunately, internet users can help give us insights into what is happening and what pieces of content are similar. This paper will go over an example of this using Wikipedia click data within the month of October, 2025. All of this data is based solely on the click behavior of users on Wikipedia.

## What Problem can this Technology Solve?
With the rapid pace of the internet, it can be difficult to group similar content together or to be able to tell what genre a new item should be. Sure there are the labels that the creator placed on their creation, but these need to be taken with a grain of salt. These labels can carry human biases or even purposefully be mislabeled to benifit specific parties. Instead, it would be much better for users and companies, at least if there are no nefarious intents, to use naturally occuring data to decide what to do with new items. In this way, it can be a large group of users and their habits that describes an item, rather than a single person's concious thought. There are a few ways to do this, but something than many companies should have available is data to tell where a user went from one page or piece of content to the next. This can be used by the Leiden Community Algorithm.
